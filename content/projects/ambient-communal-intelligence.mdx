Ambient Communal Intelligence (ACI) is a proposal for using intelligence technology to help groups of people coordinate. 

![The essay's header image: a dotted dome over a low modernist city at night.](/project-media/ambient-communal-intelligence/01-beaa9dc6b1.webp)

It is ambient because it stays present but in the background, never replacing the parts of coordination that need human care and creativity; communal because it is built to reflect the values of the community and the members it represents; and intelligent because it runs on large language models.

The essay sketches the roles such a system could play: navigation through what a community knows, context about who people are and what they seek, connection between unlikely collaborators, curation of everything a community produces into digests, and administration that quietly takes on the busywork. It rests on three pillars: a central resource directory, identity and governance, and managed agentic infrastructure.

It also names the hard parts: how much process a community will tolerate, physical spaces the system cannot see, out-of-date and conflicting information, over-assumption about people, and privacy.
